**What makes the work worth publishing.**

1. **The evaluation study has a useful, concrete result.** Changing evaluation and training choices reverses 25-34% of generator-pair rankings, and the decomposition identifies an optimization-budget explanation for the large training effect. These results can help readers design more credible synthetic-data comparisons. The value lies in the demonstrated consequences and their causes, rather than claiming that each individual precaution is new.
2. **The relationship study produces a practical methodological lesson.** Exact reported formulas and approximate laboratory relationships require different handling. The new controls explain why enforcing zero residuals can be wrong for the observed blood-count measurements, and why preserving individual residual distributions alone does not preserve their joint behavior.
3. **Simple controls now strengthen the argument.** Copula or TabDiff outputs with reconstruction or joint residual resampling expose what is contributed by relation knowledge, joint sampling and learned generation. Showing where these simple approaches succeed is a useful result, even where it reduces the apparent advantage of MedHyperDiff.
4. **There is a modest remaining learned-model benefit.** In the five robustness blood-count settings, MedHyperDiff has the lowest mean relation distance, with margins of approximately 0.008-0.019 over the best control. It also has the lowest eligible mean C2ST in five of ten robustness settings. These are worthwhile observations when described at their actual scale.
5. **The reporting is substantially more candid and inspectable.** Negative results, the failed n = 100 privacy diagnostics, the n = 2,000 reversals, local protocol status, post hoc additions, configuration details and source snapshots are now visible. This improves credibility independently of whether the proposed model wins.

**The scientific limitations that still matter.**

**Novelty is moderate and mainly methodological.** The current evidence supports a study of evaluation choices and residual representations. The connection to prior dependent-feature reconstruction is now acknowledged. The paper does not isolate the value of the final graph/hypergraph design. In Table IX, blood-count coupling error is approximately 0.068 for MedHyperDiff, 0.061 for copula with joint residuals, and 0.062 for TabDiff with joint residuals. The residual-to-covariate dependence diagnostic also does not establish an advantage for MedHyperDiff. A reviewer can reasonably ask why the learned model is needed; the defensible answer is its small observed marginal-residual and selected realism advantages, with costs and limitations, rather than a unique ability to preserve coupling.

**The five wins are ranks of means, with uneven replication across samples.** For blood counts 2021-23 at n = 1,000, C2ST is 0.6291 versus 0.6340 for TabDiff with joint residuals, but MedHyperDiff wins in only two of five paired samples. The median paired difference is +0.00335, favoring the comparator because lower C2ST is better. The current detailed results acknowledge this narrow case. The abstract and conclusion should preserve that distinction. Neither the ten settings nor the overlapping repeated samples provide ten independent population-level confirmations.

**Comparative fairness and fresh confirmation remain limited.** The proposed model's budget was selected during development, whereas comparator configurations were fixed. The added textbook and joint-resampling controls use already examined confirmation outputs. Both choices are now disclosed, but disclosure does not make the resulting model ranking a tuning-matched, untouched evaluation. This is the most consequential remaining limitation if the authors want a stronger claim of generator superiority.

**Clinical usefulness remains only partially evaluated.** Relation-dependent clinical categories favor MedHyperDiff in four of ten settings and favor a comparator in six. Utility uses a single prediction task and one learner, with small improvements at the smaller sizes and no consistent advantage at n = 2,000. A smaller residual distance therefore cannot be equated with a clinically better synthetic cohort. The paper's current restricted methods framing can accommodate this limitation; a deployment or clinical-benefit claim would require a specified use case and corresponding validation.

**Generality and privacy are bounded.** All relation datasets come from NHANES, with panel overlap, unweighted analyses and a limited set of laboratory relationships. Hospital workflows, different analyzers, longitudinal records and other missingness patterns remain untested. Membership AUROC is an empirical diagnostic, and a mean threshold of 0.60 is neither a privacy guarantee nor a guarantee for every individual run. The current paper generally says this appropriately.

Bundling the CSV or supporting registry-based table generation would address it without releasing all large run artifacts. The summary's statement that every manuscript table row is generated by code is broader than what I verified: the final reporting script directly writes the robustness and diagnostic LaTeX rows. Document the actual commands for the remaining tables as well. This is a request for a demonstrable regeneration path, not a finding that their numbers are wrong.

Visual inspection found no major rendering defect that prevents reading the paper. Figure 7 is dense, Table VI interrupts the discussion around Equation 3, and the 13-page conference layout should be adapted to the selected journal. A short decision table distinguishing exact reported formulas, approximate formulas with dependent residuals, and relationships with important covariate-dependent residuals would make the methodological contribution easier to apply. These are presentation improvements rather than grounds to reject the results.

**Additional experiments should follow the intended claim.** I would not make every previously suggested experiment a condition for submitting this narrower paper.

| Intended improvement | Most useful next work | What it would establish |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger comparative evidence | Freeze preprocessing, reconstruction, budgets and metrics; tune the leading comparators under a declared resource budget; evaluate once on genuinely untouched data, using participant-consistent splits | Whether the modest learned-model gains survive fair selection and fresh evaluation |
| Architectural novelty | Matched final-model ablation with and without graph/hypergraph, holding representation, training and capacity as comparable as practicable | Whether the architecture adds value beyond residual coding |
| Clinical usefulness | Choose a concrete clinical/research use, then evaluate its relevant outcomes, calibration, subgroups and category behavior with domain input | Whether synthetic-data differences matter for that use |
| Broader applicability | A non-NHANES cohort with different measurement or missingness properties | Evidence beyond this data source |

If only one substantial new study is feasible, prioritize the first row. An external cohort would strengthen it further, but a smaller, genuinely untouched and fairly tuned comparison is already informative. Additional seeds on the same examined reference data do not substitute for it. If the current contribution remains an empirical account of evaluation and relation handling, the architecture ablation is optional; it becomes necessary for a renewed claim that the hypergraph itself is the source of the gain.
